Scour & Sorcery

A long-abandoned academy of magic is reopening, and you are its new caretaker. Restore it wing by wing with spells instead of a pressure washer — and find out why it closed.

In development — tech prototype (M0)

The pitch

Each restored wing comes back to life: students, staff, and creatures move in. They bring magical mishaps that you clean up quietly, before the headmaster notices. While you restore the academy, you uncover the story of why it was sealed.

Comparables: PowerWash Simulator 2 (satisfying cleaning) meets Crime Scene Cleaner (hidden-residue reveal, cover-up tension), set in a cozy magic-school castle. Original IP.

Core loop

  1. Restore an abandoned wing with wand spells (Beam, Fan, Cone, Burst). Use solvents on stubborn residue and a revealing charm to find hidden stains.
  2. Reopen. The wing fills with life — a shop or a class — and unlocks new upgrades.
  3. Incidents. Small, optional new messes (an exploded cauldron, a runaway self-stirring pot) carry the best rewards. A gnome crew cleans up any you skip, for less pay.
  4. Mystery. Some restorations open sealed rooms that move the main story forward.

Why it works on PlayStation 5

  • DualSense: adaptive-trigger resistance for spell pressure, and haptics that change per residue (grit, slime, soot). You feel the completion "ding".
  • Short, satisfying sessions that also suit long, relaxed play.
  • Clear per-object, per-room, and per-wing progress — a natural fit for Trophies (100% wings, Base-Kit-Only runs, hidden collectibles).

Technology

  • Engine: Unity 6 (URP) — the same engine as both cleaning comparables.
  • Core tech: per-object UV dirt masks, GPU brush painting via compute shaders, and completion % computed from mip-reduced masks with AsyncGPUReadback — no per-frame CPU readback.
  • Data-driven content: levels, incidents, gnomes, and economy are defined in JSON, so one developer can ship and balance content without engine changes.
